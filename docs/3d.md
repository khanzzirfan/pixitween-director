# 3D product shots

A scene can show the real product turning in studio light, with a soft shadow on the plate, instead of a flat cut-out sliding around. The product is the user's own photo wrapped on a 3D shape, not a model drawn by an AI, so the label text stays exactly as it was photographed.

3D is off until `PRODUCT_3D=true` is set, and needs the engine's `model3d` layer (`reacthub-pixitweenjs` 0.3.0-alpha.5 or newer).

## Motion, 3D or Generate

The director (and the showrunner of the crew) picks the cheapest technique that can tell each shot, and says why on the scene's card.

| Mode | Use it when | How it is made | Cost |
|------|-------------|----------------|------|
| Motion | Most shots: lifestyle, text-led, parallax over a plate | 2D layers, a cut-out of the product, camera moves | cents of model calls |
| 3D | The product itself turns, catches the light or is revealed, and it is rigid packaging (can, bottle, box, jar, tube, pouch) | One `model3d` layer wearing the photo's pixels | nothing to render, edits are instant |
| Generate | Physics a still cannot fake: pouring, hands, cloth, people | A video clip from a video model, with the user's approval | about 25 credits a clip |

In the classic studio every scene has a three-way switch in the Generate tab (Motion, 3D, Generate); in a crew film, ask the showrunner and it changes the scene's mode and says why. Motion and 3D swap for free, with no model call: switching to 3D builds the layer from the product already in the scene, and switching back restores the original product layer exactly as it was. The storyboard also records the shape of the product (`productShape`): `can`, `bottle`, `box`, `jar`, `tube`, `pouch`, or `other` for anything else, which is extruded from its outline.

In chat, "turn it more" and "softer light from the window" are one tool call each (`turnProduct`, `lightProduct`) and apply at once.

## A label from the photo

The cut-out of the product, with the background removed, is the only image the 3D shape needs. For a round product (can, bottle, jar, tube) it is unwrapped into a 2048 by 1024 label:

1. **The straight body.** Rows whose width stays within 4 % of the commonest width are the body. Shoulders, necks, lids and rims fall outside it. A cone or a pyramid has no such body and falls back to an extruded cut-out.
2. **Unproject.** Each column of the label is an angle around the body. Within 70 degrees either side of the front, the label takes the photo's pixels (column = centre + radius * sin(angle)). At 70 degrees the surface is seen at a third of its width, so pixels there are stretched about three times and detail turns to smear beyond it.
3. **The sides nobody photographed** take a side colour, from the median colour of the columns nearest each edge of the body, with a 12 degree feather so there is no seam. When the two edges differ, the colour that spreads further in wins.

Boxes and pouches keep the photo as the front face. Odd shapes get an outline of the cut-out (marching along the mask's edge, then simplified to at most 400 points) and the photo, cropped to the outline's box, as the front cap. The image work runs once per product per film and is cached.

## Why 35 degrees

The product turns 18 degrees either side of the front by default, and a chat edit can ask for at most 35.

Only the front was photographed. At 18 degrees the part that was not photographed appears between 52 and 90 degrees from the viewer, where it is drawn at 62 % of its width or less and the key light falls off toward the rim, so the side colour is hard to see. At 35 degrees it appears from 55 degrees, at up to 82 % width, and the filled band becomes plainly visible. These are geometry from the unwrap above, not measurements of the look: whether 35 is acceptable on real footage is judged by eye in the live check below.

## The model3d layer

A 3D product is one layer of the engine's own type, `model3d`, written into the scene next to the 2D layers, so it shares their order, camera moves, review and export. It has:

- a viewport (the layer's own off-screen rectangle) at twice the product's box for crisp edges, never more than 2048 on a side;
- a source: a primitive (`can`, `bottle`, `box`, `jar`, `tube`, `pouch`) or an extruded outline, with the label or front as a texture and the side colour;
- a turn: rotation keys from `[4, -18, 0]` to `[4, 18, 0]` degrees across the scene, eased. The 4 degree tilt shows the top rim, which reads as 3D at once;
- a light preset (`studio`, `window`, `golden`, `night`), a soft shadow, and a camera with 50 % headroom around the product.

A few things were found by looking at the engine's output in a browser (headless Chrome, 30 October 2026, engine 0.3.0-alpha.5):

- The layer is clipped at its viewport edge, and so is its shadow. A low key light throws a shadow along the plate that is cut flat at that edge, with the golden and the studio presets at their own angles. The key light therefore starts high (75 degrees elevation, 20 degrees to the left), which keeps the shadow under the product; `lightProduct` can change it.
- Draw order is the layer order: a headline placed before the product is covered by it when it turns, and a caption placed after stays in front.
- Seeking to the same time twice gives the same picture (the same hash of the canvas), so captures and exports are repeatable.
- The review rules measure the product itself, not its headroom, so text over a 3D product is flagged like text over a flat one.

Capture and export wait for the engine to say every 3D layer has drawn (`onRenderReady`) before they take a frame: a review capture waits up to 10 seconds and then goes on, noting it; an export waits up to 20 seconds and then stops with a message instead of recording an empty frame.
